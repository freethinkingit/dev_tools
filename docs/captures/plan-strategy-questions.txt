  1. January to December 2027, reviewed each quarter.
  2. Today about 40 engineers on one team use it for runbook and dashboard links. By the end of 2027 it should serve the whole company, around 600 people, including support and sales, who share links with customers.
  3. Goals: (a) Company-wide adoption: 400 weekly active creators by Q4; worth the most, it is why we fund it. (b) Links never break: 99.9% of redirects succeed each month, measured from server logs; second, because a broken link in a customer email costs trust. (c) Owners can see and fix their links: every link has an owner and an edit page, measured as zero ownerless links at the Q4 review; third.
  4. Current state: one Python process on a single VM, SQLite file, no auth, no backups, about 3,000 links and 20,000 redirects a month. The store and redirect work and must be kept; codes are random with no collision check, there is no way to edit or delete a link, and the README serve command is wrong.
  5. Principles: redirects stay under 50 ms at p99; links shared with customers must never expose internal URLs to people outside the company without an explicit public flag; we run on the company Kubernetes platform and Postgres, no new vendors; two engineers, half time.
  6. Please propose streams from the goals.
  7. Risks: SSO integration slips because the identity team is busy (likely, high impact; start the request in Q1 and ship read-only creation behind VPN meanwhile). Migration from SQLite loses links (unlikely, high impact; dual-write and compare for a month). Adoption stalls because people keep using docs bookmarks (medium, medium; browser extension and Slack unfurls).
  8. Out of scope: analytics beyond click counts (product analytics owns that), custom vanity domains (next year), a public URL shortener for the internet (not our job).
