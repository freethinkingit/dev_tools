● Three of your answers contradict the platform move design you approved. This design follows your answers, so the two documents now disagree, and the reviewer marks it as a blocker:
  - **Copy order.** The platform move design says the one-time copy runs "before dual-write begins". You chose dual-write first, then the copy.
  - **The SQLite store.** The platform move design says "The SQLite store is the existing store, kept as it is". Your answer changes its `add` to take a code. The design also adds `links` and `insert_missing` to it, and opens it read-only after cutover.
  - **Rollback.** The platform move design's rollback is pointing `go.internal` back at the VM, with SQLite read-only. Your answer adds a copy-back and makes SQLite writable again.

  There are two ways to settle this:
  - **Update the platform move design to match.** It gets a short revision, then a review, and you approve it again. This design stays as written.
  - **Revert this design to the platform move design's order and rules.** The platform move design stays as approved.

  I recommend updating the platform move design. Your answers close gaps it left open, such as how a rollback keeps the links created after cutover. Say which you want in the free text.

  I wrote two points myself; your answers didn't state them:
  - **The order of writes.** The dual-write store writes SQLite first and Postgres second, so a failed Postgres write never fails a create.
  - **Creation times of existing links.** Copied links get the copy's time as `created_at`, since SQLite holds no creation time.
